Ferrari Enzo

The Machine

Introduced in 2002, the Ferrari Enzo was a limited-production supercar built to celebrate the engineering spirit of Ferrari’s founder. It served as Ferrari's flagship model until the LaFerrari took over in March of 2013. Its naturally aspirated 6.0-litre V12 produces 660 CV (485 kW) and 657 Nm of torque, delivered to the rear wheels through a six-speed, Formula 1-inspired automated manual gearbox. With a top speed of around 350 km/h, it combined extraordinary performance with a carbon-fibre chassis, carbon-ceramic brakes and aerodynamic styling by Pininfarina designer Ken Okuyama. Ferrari originally planned 349 cars, but demand led to 399 customer cars; a 400th was built for Pope John Paul II and auctioned for charity.
