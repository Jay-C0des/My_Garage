Ferrari 365 GTB/4 (Daytona)

The Machine

The Ferrari 365 GTB/4 Daytona was introduced at the 1968 Paris Motor Show as Ferrari's flagship front-engined grand tourer. Designed by Pininfarina and powered by a 4.4-litre naturally aspirated V12 producing 352 hp (259 kW), it sent power to the rear wheels through a five-speed manual gearbox and could reach around 280 km/h. Its long bonnet, sweeping fastback profile and muscular stance gave it a distinctive presence, while its competition versions proved remarkably capable in endurance racing. Privateer-entered Daytonas achieved three consecutive GT-class victories at Le Mans from 1972 to 1974, cementing the car's reputation as both a refined road-going GT and a formidable racing machine.

The Collector

In case of Daytona, or 365 GTB/4 as it is officially christened, I find the history more appealing than the model. Fans and journalists named it after Ferrari achieved its famous 1-2-3 podium finish in 1967 at the 24 hours of Daytona, a premier endurance race held annually. The 365 GTB/4 wasn't even produced back then yet, it took the name. It would later go on to win Le Mans, as I've mentioned before. The things I love about this model is the long nose and the headlights, which are not painted. Good job Hot Wheels.
